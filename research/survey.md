# Survey: what's getting in the way

**Source:** Product@GT survey, n=70 Georgia Tech students, Oct–Nov 2026.
Percentages below are students who rated the challenge 4 or 5 out of 5.
Quotes are verbatim from open-ended responses.

## Results

| Challenge | % rating 4–5 of 5 | n of 70 |
|---|---|---|
| Standing out among applicants | 80% | 56 |
| Getting industry experience | 66% | 46 |
| Knowing what employers expect | 61% | 43 |
| Finding mentors or guidance | 47% | 33 |

## Quotes

On standing out:
> "Putting the exact questions... an interviewer or online assessment will ask
> for a specific company and role."

On knowing what employers expect. This is the strongest evidence we have for
the core thesis, volunteered without being asked about mentorship or Skills
specifically:
> "Giving a roadmap based on my current skills and what the employers expect
> from me for that role."

On industry experience:
> "Having a concrete outcome which I can use on my resume and talk about in an
> interview."

On mentors:
> "They could connect me with industry professionals and give me personalized
> feedback on what I can improve."

## What this data does and doesn't establish

**Establishes:** a majority of surveyed students rate standing out among
applicants as a significant challenge, and a notable minority name access to
mentors or industry professionals as part of the solution, unprompted.

**Does not establish:**

- **That respondents are Google Skills users.** Not confirmed whether the
  survey screened for Skills usage. If the sample is not on Skills, the
  "current skills" context that is the core of our argument doesn't exist
  for them yet.
- **That students want this specifically on Google Skills.** The quote "they
  could connect me with industry professionals" answers a question about
  needs, not about where that connection should happen. If the survey
  prompt named Google Skills, that's stronger; if generic, it isn't evidence
  for the platform choice.
- **Recruitment and selection bias.** If respondents were reached through the
  Product@GT network, the sample likely skews toward students already
  career-focused and plugged into a professional community, which would
  inflate all four numbers relative to the general student population.
- **Forum trust or distrust.** No survey question or external source
  establishes that students distrust Google's existing community forum
  specifically. This remains an unverified assumption behind the "why not a
  forum" argument. See [`landscape.md`](landscape.md) for what's actually
  known.

## Recommended next step

Before further build investment, re-run the open-text question with the
platform named explicitly ("would you want this inside Google Skills, or
somewhere else?") and confirm how many respondents have a Skills account.
